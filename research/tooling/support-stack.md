## Product constraints, effort and exit

### Documentation: Read the Docs Community

The current Community offer is for public repositories and public documentation;
it is supported by ads. Business is a separate commercial product. [D1]
Community builds have published limits of **15 minutes, 7 GB memory, two concurrent
builds and a 5 GB disk soft limit**; the publisher can consider increases, which
have not been requested. Build output can also be uploaded from another CI. [D2]
The terms retain author ownership, require upload rights and allow hosting/indexing;
Community content is public. Free accounts need no payment information; a paid
upgrade is a separate billed action. Provider-hosted ads and trademark rules are
not an endorsement agreement. [D3]

Maintainer work would include a documentation build configuration, dependency
pins, version pruning, broken-link review and failed-build diagnosis. Keep Markdown,
configuration and source references in Git; retain the built public site and any
required redirect mapping before exit. Moving a domain or search index has work
even when the prose exports. No migration, DNS change or extra CI was performed.
Current signup is offered; no grant application or award deadline applies to this
standard product. Actual account and implementation effort remain Unknown.

### Observability: Sentry

The public Developer plan is **USD 0**, limited to **one user**, and publishes
5,000 errors, 5 GB logs, five million spans, 50 replays and a 30-day lookback in its
comparison. The paid plans and a temporary new-user replay promotion are separate;
none is used as a permanent donated entitlement here. [O1]

The **Sentry OSS sponsorship** has its own public form and discretionary admission.
Its page describes Business features, no fixed term limit and optional on-demand
budget. The linked current FAQ specifies monthly sponsored quotas, including
five million errors, 5,000 GB logs, one billion spans, 100,000 replays and five
Seer contributor seats; sponsorship is per organization, not each team. Profit-making
social enterprises/B-Corps are excluded from that program. The separate nonprofit
route asks for registered 501(c)(3) status. [O2–O3] Neither account state nor exact
offer is known here; no Sentry AI feature is selected or executed.

The terms place SDK configuration/data rights with the customer, distinguish
Service Data from website-account privacy, permit specified data uses and provide
export during the subscription. No-charge products can be terminated; absent a
designated duration the general terms use 30 days. The sponsorship's stated
duration is therefore an offer condition to preserve, not an irrevocable promise.
On-demand use and paid renewal can create charges. Technical/performance
confidentiality and customer-publicity provisions mean service comparison or
branding rights cannot be assumed. [O4] Website privacy has separate collection,
retention and international-transfer rules. [O5]

A future implementation must minimize data before transmission: exclude secrets,
private source, raw user Builds and unnecessary user identifiers. Server-side
scrubbing is a documented tool, not evidence that the SDK never transmits those
fields. [O6] Start with error metadata and explicit sampling/stop settings; a
session-replay feature should not become the default merely because it is bundled.
Maintain local diagnostic authority and exported incident records; verify actual
export scope and retention before canceling. An export right alone is not a
tested archive of all events, alerts or dashboards. Actual monthly effort, charge,
data residency, accepted operator and application decision remain Unknown.

### Localization: Crowdin OSS

The public request requires an existing translation project, an OSI license,
public source, project leadership, at least three months' work, active collaborators,
current news and regular builds, with no related commercial product. Submission
also agrees to beta participation and contribution of project translations to
**Global TM**. [L1] These conditions cannot be replaced by nonprofit status.
The pricing FAQ describes unlimited projects/strings/members for granted OSS
licenses; exact account/add-on capacity remains subject to the granted plan. [L2]

The general terms retain client-data ownership, but §12.6 gives both Crowdin and
other clients a perpetual, irrevocable, nonexclusive, royalty-free license to
public-project content, including redistribution, adaptation and derivative works,
unless a written agreement with the respective party provides otherwise. Section
18.3 keeps those rights in effect after termination. This is broader than hosting
or the separate Global TM consent. [L3]

Managed AI and BYOK have different responsibility rules. Terms §5.4 and §18.3 give
inconsistent account-deletion timing, so this note promises no single retention period.
Export before deletion; obtain clarification before relying on post-termination
availability. Section 5.6 also permits retroactive quota true-ups or invoices and
separate usage charges for CDN, credits and paid apps, including collection from a
balance/payment method. Paid renewal rules remain separate. Section 2 requires
prior written consent for service monitoring, benchmarking or competitive use;
this source assessment authorizes no such experiment. [L3]

Personal-data duties and specialized-model exceptions in
the privacy policy further prevent a universal “never used for training” claim.
[L4]

File-based translations can be downloaded as configured ZIP/language/file outputs;
string-based projects instead use target-file bundles. [L5] Keep resource keys,
plural/placeholder checks, approved translations and provenance in Git. A future
maintainer must review changes, resolve terminology and obtain real language review;
hosting does not supply translators or demonstrate accessibility. Before exit,
export the required formats and separately verify whether translation memories,
glossaries, approval history and attribution are included. No current translator
count, import, export, account, deadline, renewal guarantee or cost saving is claimed.

### Design: Penpot Professional

The public pricing page lists **USD 0 per user/month** for Professional and separate
paid plans. Nonprofit/OSS discounts are advertised separately; no discount program
has been qualified here. The flattened comparison did not securely bind every
storage/history/member value to a plan, and a direct HTML follow-up returned 403:
those exact quota columns remain **Unknown**, not “unlimited” by inference. [G1]
The service terms cover account authority, fair use, asset/font rights and host
processing; owning a font locally does not prove permission to upload or share it.
The terms' minimum age and privacy page's under-18 language differ; this is
unresolved for any minor-user proposal, which is not selected here. [G2–G3]

The documented current export is a `.penpot` ZIP with JSON and binary assets;
shared-library export options affect linkage. The import guide states a 1 GB
limit, and native exports use the current v3 format rather than the deprecated
binary format. [G4] Keep editable exports, source fonts/assets and rendered
reference images with their rights and version pins. A successful import into
an independently controlled compatible instance would be needed to establish
the actual exit path; this research did not execute it. Source availability does
not make operating a self-hosted design service free of work or infrastructure.

The current minimal selection adds **no proposed paid subscription**; it does not
establish that the organization's existing bills are zero.
