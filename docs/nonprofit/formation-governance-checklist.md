## 2. What the official sources establish

The IRS distinguishes state nonprofit status from federal income-tax exemption. Its preparation guidance identifies organizational form and documents, state registration analysis and an EIN as separate inputs. **Incorporation, EIN assignment, recognized federal exemption and eligibility for a specific support program are separate determinations.** See S1 and S4 below.

For the federal organizing test, an organization's purposes must be limited appropriately and its assets dedicated to exempt purposes. S3 describes the purpose and dissolution provisions, including limited situations in which state law can supply the asset-dedication requirement. Do not assume an unknown state's default law supplies a missing provision. Drafting an internal policy does not by itself establish that the actual organizing document meets that test.

The IRS describes bylaws as internal operating rules and says federal tax law generally does not prescribe their specific language; state law may require nonprofit corporations to have them (S6). Its current conflict-of-interest explanation recommends a policy addressing disclosure and removal from conflicted votes (S7). Those recommendations do not remove applicable legal duties, nor do they establish this project's adopted rules.

S4 instructs an organization to be legally formed before applying for an EIN. S5 requires an actual qualified responsible party and excludes nominees from applying or being listed as that party. Verify an existing EIN before considering a new request; S8 also warns against applying more than once. An EIN request is a real administrative action, not a research shortcut.

S2 explains that charitable solicitation, periodic reports, paid fundraising activity, local registration and holding charitable assets can raise separate state or local obligations. The incorporation state is not a complete fundraising-jurisdiction analysis. S9 supplies an official directory for finding the relevant agencies; it does not establish which laws apply to Pick Your AI.

## 6. Sequence for a concrete decision packet

1. **Reconcile the actual baseline.** Identify an existing organization or host if evidence establishes one; otherwise preserve Unknown. Define the specific near-term activity or resource need that motivates a structure decision.
2. **Decide which route needs preparation.** Receive the reviewed #13 comparison and actual organizational decision. A hosted project may have different formation needs from a proposed independent organization; inspect the actual agreement and applicable law rather than requiring a new entity by default.
3. **Complete the jurisdiction worksheet.** Establish the relevant facts, official sources and unresolved legal questions. Include fundraising and operational jurisdictions as well as the formation jurisdiction.
4. **Prepare and review exact documents.** Combine the supported purpose, organizing provisions, governance design, initial budget, rights inventory and control assignments. Obtain qualified legal or financial review for the specific questions that require it; an in-session engineering review supplies no legal opinion.
5. **Record authorized adoption and actual formation.** Only the competent authorized people carry out a covered decision. Retain each exact adopted document and the agency's actual outcome safely. A failed or uncertain submission remains an unresolved result until reconciled, not permission to duplicate it.
6. **Resolve EIN and operational authority.** Follow the actual legally formed entity's identity and responsible-party evidence. Separately establish accounts, custody, signers and financial implementation; do not treat an EIN receipt as a banking or contract mandate.
7. **Hand over recurring obligations.** Give #16 the actual dates and documents needed for its exemption and compliance analysis. Give the [operating procedure](../operations-and-reporting.md) any accepted real obligations. Keep the present state of every action and its evidence distinct from research completion.

These steps prepare the next decision; they do not add another permission request for code, reviews, commits or other project work already authorized. Actual organizational roles remain Unassigned until evidence identifies them.
