## Concrete public-benefit proposal and value method

The proposed use is **one bounded maintenance and public-evidence package** for the six repositories: select a current canonical issue, pin its accepted input, specify the finite correction or evidence output, retain the original validation and independent review, and publish the permitted result. It maps to needs N03, N07 and N08. An actual issue/cohort, responsible person, delivery period, unmet resource quantity and budget must still be selected before making a fundraising representation. This is an internal proposal, not a campaign promise or proof of an unfunded shortage.

Existing public CI, hosting and completed checks are the baseline. Do not ask donors to fund a duplicate implementation or infer a compute shortage from queued or failed tests. Physical-device coverage, empirical Research #61/#62 inputs and participant evidence keep their existing owners and acceptance requirements. A donation cannot establish their missing observations.

For each feasible route, compare the same period and output:

`net spendable cash = settled receipts − applicable host/platform charges − processor/FX/outgoing costs − refunds/chargebacks/reserves − actual fulfillment/administration costs`.

Reconcile the fee bases and timing before calculating: a host's deposit may already exclude an upstream charge. Donor tips and donor-paid recurring fees do not become project receipts. Restricted cash is not unrestricted spending authority; a pledged goal is not settled cash. A service's avoided cost needs an evidenced necessary alternative actually avoided. Award probability, cash amount, currency, usable value, effort hours and numeric score remain null. No conversion from credit face value, volunteer time, repository activity or provider marketing totals supplies these inputs.

## Campaign model, intake and continuing obligations

### GoFundMe: donation collection

The public start landing page was read; it describes creation, sharing and transfers before the goal is reached. It is not the authenticated campaign editor, and no actual consent fields were inspected. Published setup/identity/organization instructions are verified public process evidence, not a submitted application. There is no fixed application round observed. Actual account-specific transfer deadline remains Unknown. [F2–F6, F10]

**Actor matters:** consumer Terms F7 apply to non-nonprofit users, including individual organizers/donors/beneficiaries; nonprofit users use F14. For users covered by F7, the 120-day transfer boundary can lead to refund/escheat consequences. Purpose accuracy, sales/return restrictions, conditional holds/refunds and compliance records apply. The UK giveaway exception does not create general reward crowdfunding. [F7]

Recurring support is optional, unavailable for fundraisers in Italy and Canada under current Help, and adds a **donor-paid 5% fee**. It does not change the organizer's ordinary transaction charges. Cancellation affects future charges, with a 24-hour timing boundary; closing the fundraiser cancels recurrence. Refunds remain a separate process. Actual recurrence, receipts and reporting commitments are not activated. [F11–F12]

F7 also grants a broad perpetual, irrevocable content license including service-related AI uses, with waiver, indemnity, liability and termination provisions. Its service/content AI-development restriction leaves **exact intended-use applicability unresolved**. These clauses are not indiscriminately assigned to the nonprofit counterparty. [F7]

**Nonprofit agreement:** the readable May 15 Pro Terms recognize properly constituted nonprofits or an actual qualifying 501(c)(3) fiscal-sponsor contract, with non-US qualifications. GoFundMe Pay is part of Pro, resolving the apparent brand mismatch. Fees/currency depend on Order Terms; a prior MSA can supersede the public Terms. [F14]

Pro permits specified donation/receipt agency and requires recipient authority, content rights, consent and private-data controls. Its own brand/content license and usage-data/AI provisions apply. Publishing benchmarks or security tests of **Pro itself** needs written consent. Material donor rewards require the stated exception/agreement; token gifts and auctions have distinct treatment. Pro incorporates F7's prohibited-content section, not F7 wholesale. Default renewal continues the term unless timely notice is given at least 30 days before expiry; subscription charges and limited fee changes may apply. Suspension can delay funds; termination need not refund fees. Assignment needs consent, and indemnity/liability/confidentiality duties matter. These public conditions do not establish PYA's actual agreement, product, recipient or costs. [F14]

The August 17 Privacy Notice distinguishes public fundraising material from private administration. Public anonymity does not hide a donor from all organizers/beneficiaries. It describes affiliate/vendor sharing, service-related AI training, cross-border processing and retention based on purpose/law; it excludes training unrelated general-purpose commercial AI products. Recipient organizations can have their own data-controller duties. Keep donor identities, bank/ID documents and private account data outside public evidence repositories. Actual roles, consent, export and deletion handling remain unverified. [F13]

### Kickstarter: creative project with possible rewards

The current Rules require human creative involvement and an actual demonstrated app/software prototype. The finite new creation must have a credible completion plan; ordinary maintenance and general nonprofit support are insufficient. Rewardless pledges are permitted, but do not remove the creative-project requirement. Up to 10% of campaign funds may go to charity if disclosed on submission; reward items can also be donated. Therefore a blanket statement that charities cannot use Kickstarter would be incorrect. [K1, K3–K4, K17]

Published intake is draft, submission/review, then launch. Design & Technology requires manual review; published review timing is not a promised approval date. Creator eligibility lists 25 launch countries with country/entity/ID/bank requirements. The start page exposed no readable form body. Actual form fields, consent and complete Terms/Privacy remain **Not Verified** because the linked legal pages returned JavaScript-disabled shells. These access observations do not prove a login wall or HTTP error. [K2, K26–K30]

Campaign duration is 1–60 days; the selected goal/deadline cannot be changed after launch. The completion budget must include production, fees, taxes and fulfillment. Optional installment and Late Pledge mechanisms have separate delayed schedules; no optional feature is assumed enabled. [K7–K11]

Creators remain responsible for promised work, rewards, honest updates and reasonable resolution efforts if completion fails. Refunds are requested from creators; after payout, a creator issuing a refund bears platform/processing fees. Budget for disputes, failed collections and promised deliveries rather than counting the displayed pledge total as unrestricted cash. [K12–K15]

AI disclosures, content rights and actual source-consent evidence need project-specific review. Help's creator-ownership wording does not establish the full platform license. Backer data is restricted to legitimate communication/fulfillment, with consent needed for other use; exported data and collaborators require controls. Report access normally expires two years after the latest estimated reward delivery date. Launched/canceled project records can persist after account deletion; cancellation is irreversible. No automatic migration or erasure is promised. [K16–K25]

## Activation order and remaining questions

1. **Qualify the recipient and finite need.** Use actual entity/jurisdiction/authority evidence and the accepted need ledger. Establish the account/host relationship, publication rights and incremental workload without appointing an absent person or inventing donor demand.
2. **Compare one existing collection facility.** Use the canonical Sponsors and host findings, current written terms, actual fee base and control/receipt responsibilities. Select an appropriate route only after those facts are available. A charity host is conditional on needing and qualifying for its services; it is not a required preliminary incorporation step.
3. **Add direct collection only when warranted.** A real organization with adequate controls may compare its own bank/processor against the host. Obtain the specific service quote, privacy/receipt/chargeback obligations and a workable exit plan before calling it cheaper.
4. **Reassess GoFundMe only for a justified additional audience/campaign.** Resolve country, recipient, beneficiary consent, partner nomenclature, intended-use/AI restrictions and complete account terms. Avoid duplicating receipt, reporting and donor-data handling already provided by a host/Sponsors.
5. **Leave Kickstarter unselected for maintenance.** A separately proposed creative deliverable, demonstrated prototype, human/source-rights evidence, actual capacity and complete legal terms could justify a new assessment. Nothing here promises rewards or authorizes campaign launch.

The remaining questions are those concrete recipient/need/terms facts; donor-specific tax treatment; actual collection/fulfillment budget and effort; host fee-base conflicts; GoFundMe's actual direct-charity payment/receipt agreement and AI-use scope; and Kickstarter's complete legal/form conditions. A numerical score remains unsupported. Resolving this research issue does not complete the related implementation, financial-control, legal-formation, participant or resource-acquisition issues.
