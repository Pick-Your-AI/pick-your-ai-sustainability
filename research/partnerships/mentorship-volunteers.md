### Capacity method and synthetic arithmetic

For planning only, let `N` be proposed participants; `W` the agreed weeks; `s` setup/closeout hours per participant; `t`, `r`, `q` weekly teaching, review and question/triage hours per participant; `a` weekly administration hours for the cohort; and `b` weekly reserved backup hours for the cohort. Then:

`required planned hours = N × [s + W × (t + r + q)] + W × (a + b)`.

Compare each person's separately evidenced available hours against the role they actually cover; do not rely only on an aggregate total that masks a single unavailable reviewer. Reserve backup capacity instead of assuming it can always be reclaimed. Re-estimate after setup, when learning needs or scope change, and before a second participant is considered. Program evaluations, accessibility support, incidents and turnover may require extra time and must fit a revised approved budget.

**Synthetic arithmetic example, not an estimate of this project or either program:** choose `N=1`, `W=12`, `s=6`, `t=2`, `r=2`, `q=1`, `a=1`, `b=1`. The illustration is `1 × [6 + 12 × 5] + 12 × 2 = 66 + 24 = 90` planned mentor/administrator hours. A hypothetical 84-hour total would be short by six hours even before assessing role coverage. It therefore would not pass the illustrative capacity test. None of those hours is a mentor commitment, contributor project duration, monetary valuation, accounting donation or observed cost saving. The actual inputs and output remain Unknown.

There is no universal “free” mentoring hour. Record who bears setup, preparation, review, accessibility and coordination costs if an actual arrangement is later selected. Keep any real cash inflow, stipend to a participant, reimbursed expense and separately supported avoided expense distinct. No hourly rate, award probability, dollar conversion or net benefit is supplied here. In particular, LFX's mentorship terms do not justify treating mentee work as a financial/commercial benefit to the project. [S18]

## 7. Teaching, inclusion and safe exit design

The following are **proposed operating choices** for later review, not actions already performed:

1. **Before invitation:** select a public, meaningful and genuinely unclaimed learning brief with its current owner. State expected skills, learning goals, supported modes, real time bounds, any stipend/expense facts, rights terms, private reporting route and evaluation rubric. Do not advertise guaranteed employment, funding or acceptance. A mentor's company affiliation supplies no authority to make an offer for that company.
2. **Before a start date:** complete the actual capacity and operational records. Explain the distinction between public contributions and private teaching/assessment information. Obtain the applicable participant permission; do not infer it from joining a repository. Agree on a workable schedule and reasonable contact window, with accessible asynchronous alternatives where possible.
3. **During setup:** walk through the supported development or evidence-reading environment using cleared inputs. Keep a no-install/no-build route for an evidence-reading exercise where it is educationally adequate. A failed setup is a real finding, not evidence of low ability. Resolve access and accommodation needs before evaluating performance against a task that assumes that access.
4. **During teaching:** use short, specific feedback and ask the participant to explain their reasoning. Preserve the first attempt, assistance supplied, revisions and final disposition. Re-scope openly when the proposed work is unsuitable. Do not demand constant availability, public personal explanations, marketing posts or artificial PR volume.
5. **At difficulty or absence:** first clarify the barrier through the agreed safe channel. Offer the pre-agreed backup or a pause; involve the independent administrator for conflict or conduct concerns. Protect private reports from the person concerned where appropriate. A conduct report is not automatically a technical-performance finding. The program's formal evaluation/dispute rules still govern any enrolled program.
6. **At completion or withdrawal:** provide an accurate skills/work handoff and distinguish completed learning from accepted code and actual downstream use. A person can leave without an implied continuing workload, testimonial, donation or recruitment obligation. Obtain separate permission before publishing identifying recognition; do not publish private explanations for leaving.

Project-specific conduct handling and real reporting contacts must exist before recruitment. This plan does not invent a safeguarding officer, a staffed response service, a grievance decision or any jurisdiction's legal requirements. It does not recruit minors: the compared program guidance discusses adults, and any future project-owned route must determine its own lawful/appropriate scope before invitation. Adult self-attestation in Research #63 is a separate selected instrument choice, not a blanket age or legal-capacity determination for mentorship. [S3, S14, S16, S22]
