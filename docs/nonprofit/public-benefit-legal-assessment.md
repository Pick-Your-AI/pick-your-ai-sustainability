## 1. Finding and decision boundary

The strongest presently supportable argument is that carefully explained, accessible AI comparison evidence **could help members of the public understand choices and limitations**. The repositories contain observable source, methods and bounded implementation artifacts that can support that program. They do not yet establish who learned, how much they benefited, the entity's actual operations, or whether those operations satisfy a tax exemption test. The scientific argument needs an actual scientific question, method and public-interest research activity; ordinary software engineering or product comparison cannot be classified by a repository name.

Open-source publication is useful evidence of availability and possible reuse. It does **not** establish 501(c)(3) eligibility on its own. A public repository, passing tests, a license, a neutral-design promise or charitable intent cannot fill the missing governing documents, actual beneficiary evidence, transactions, decision rights or operating facts. The [accepted entity baseline](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-sustainability/blob/405f25178a2ef27396b284089114131f7b25d9f0/docs/nonprofit/entity-baseline.md) remains the source of those Unknowns.

**Program/opportunity, intake round, opportunity stage and weighted award score: Not applicable.** This foundational issue evaluates a purpose argument, not a grant or provider application. A future exemption filing has a separate process, eligibility and entity-dependent timing; none is selected or completed here. This is P1 because a defensible external narrative depends on separating actual activity from desired benefit.

### Official framework used

These concise rules frame the evidence questions; they do not decide the project's facts.

- The IRS requires both organizational and operational qualification, with an exempt purpose and restrictions on private interests, inurement and political/lobbying activity. A beneficial product alone does not answer that organizational inquiry. [L1]
- Education includes developing capabilities and useful public instruction. A supported presentation should enable an independent conclusion. Scientific research must serve a scientific purpose and public interest; ordinary commercial testing/design is excluded. The regulation distinguishes public-availability, government and specified public-directed research routes, including qualified sponsor-IP cases. [L2, §(d)(3), (d)(5)]
- Governing purposes and dissolution assets need suitable treatment; more than insubstantial nonexempt activity presents an operational problem. Related business is not categorically prohibited, but primary unrelated business presents a separate organization-level issue. No numerical safe harbor is inferred. [L2, §(a)–(c), (e)]
- Private benefit and insider inurement require separate attention. Personal/private interests include more than formal shareholders. An apparently reasonable payment does not itself establish that the overall arrangement serves the public. [L3; L2, §(d)(1)(iii), example 3]
- For most exempt organizations, unrelated-business analysis considers business activity, regularity and relationship to the exempt purpose, with applicable exceptions. Recognition does not eliminate possible tax and filing duties. Paying a tax is not this project's evidence of organizational qualification. [L4, L5]
