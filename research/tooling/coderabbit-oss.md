### Permissions and operating boundary

The current GitHub guide requires repository owner access and organization owner access for organization authorization. Login access includes organization/team and email reads. Its documented app grant includes reads of Actions, discussions, members, metadata and merge queues, plus **write access to code, checks, commit statuses, issues, PRs and workflows**. [S4]

No GitHub Actions workflow, required check or bypass policy should be changed merely to test review usefulness.

The public-repository route cannot be represented as a no-training service. The privacy notice updated **October 16, 2026** expressly excludes OSS from section 7's no-training assurance and says CodeRabbit trains its systems on OSS. [S6] The FAQ's customer-code assurances open with proprietary-code confidentiality, while the Terms of Service dated **April 20, 2026**, section 3.3, generally say neither CodeRabbit nor its model providers train AI models on Customer's code. [S5][S7] These differing scopes remain unresolved for an actual PYA agreement; no account-specific override or applicable OSS training opt-out was observed. A pilot's input/rights review must address that OSS use before any activation.

The separate caching page states that reusable source/dependency caches are enabled by default, expire within seven days and can be disabled with `reviews.disable_cache`. Its encryption statement expressly excludes OSS projects. Do not describe this route as universally encrypted caching or zero retention. Cache control and knowledge-base retention are distinct settings. [S10]

The privacy notice's section 12 is labeled for proprietary-code retention and describes purpose/legal retention criteria and an opt-out of review-improvement storage. That storage control establishes no OSS training opt-out or removal from prior training. [S6] Before a pilot, assess the published training scope and authority over all proposed source/PR/log inputs, then inventory effective retention, logging and connected-context controls. ## Useful work and a defensible value method

The project already has GitHub review and CI capabilities, documented in the [accepted GitHub baseline](github-baseline.md). A review assistant could add reproducible findings on cross-file source/data invariants; it cannot supply research source verification, native-device execution, release rights, signed provenance or another contributor's acceptance. Existing technical issue ownership stays in its repository.

If adoption gates are resolved, the smallest useful pilot is a bounded set of ordinary sustainability Python/JSON changes under its existing [validation process](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#local-validation). This repository offers a limited scope for comparing suggestions with reproducible failures and accepted fixes. The other five repositories remain in the coverage inventory, with any later rollout decided by their maintainers from actual results.

Prepare the pilot protocol before any service use:

1. Record the selected PR revisions, effective app/configuration scope, observation window, stop condition and applicable terms. Resolve the OSS-training/input-rights assessment before transmitting any material. Include every selected PR, including skipped or rate-limited reviews, so coverage has a denominator.
2. Retain existing independent reviews and tests. Classify each distinct bot finding as reproduced and useful, already caught, unsupported, or out of scope, with its evidence and disposition. Deduplicate repeated findings and track review delays and unavailable capacity.
3. Record aggregate service-evaluation effort: setup, triage, validation, integration and continued administration. Do not turn these observations into individual performance or employment metrics. Obtain the needed rights determination before any public benchmark or comparative report.
4. Report added useful findings and actual costs separately. Net usable value depends on a documented baseline and observed additional effort avoided, less setup/triage/maintenance costs. Volunteer time is not automatically cash saved; a free offer is not avoided subscription spend without evidence that such spending would otherwise occur.
5. Stop or keep the existing workflow if useful incremental coverage and acceptable burden are not demonstrated. A bot approval never substitutes for independent acceptance or an applicable check.

Count coverage only when an actual delivered review is tied to the selected revision. A rate-limited push can produce a passing check and retain an earlier approval even though no review ran; the rate-limit comment records that absence. A green check or retained approval alone is insufficient execution evidence. [S3]

No pilot has run, so useful findings, failure rates, hours, avoided costs, benefit lifetime and selection likelihood are **Unknown**. The documented automatic-review controls can later support an opt-in/manual scope and limit repeated incremental reviews, but no configuration is changed here. [S9]
