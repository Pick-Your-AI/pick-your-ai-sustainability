## 2. Two charitable regimes, two applicability decisions

Assess charitable-trust registration under [ORC 109.26](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-109.26) separately from charitable-solicitation registration under [ORC 1716.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1716.02). A documented exemption under one regime does not establish exemption under the other. [Ohio AGO registration guidance](https://charitable.ohioago.gov/charity-registration), checked 2026-10-23, describes the exemption-request process and evidence requirements.

The small-organization solicitation provision in [ORC 1716.03(G)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1716.03) is not a blanket “under $25,000 means exempt” rule. Its gross-revenue test includes stated exclusions for governmental and 501(c)(3) grants/awards and limits compensation for a person primarily to solicit; the statute includes a filing rule after the threshold is crossed. Other exemptions have different facts, including the separate continuous-existence and federal/trust conditions in division (B). Actual receipts, paid solicitors, activities and exemption evidence are Unknown.

For annual trust reporting, [OAC 109:1-1-04](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-109:1-1-04) uses gross-receipt/asset thresholds and an asset-based fee schedule. The agency's guidance still directs smaller organizations to provide basic information through its system. [OAC 109:1-2-02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-109:1-2-02) separately governs solicitation annual reporting and contribution-based fees. Under [ORC 1716.02(D)(3)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1716.02), that fee is based on contributions received from persons in Ohio. If the exact Ohio amount cannot be determined from the records, the statute requires an estimate of Ohio contributions, explained in writing when the fee is paid and substantiated if the Attorney General requests it. Do not collapse the two denominators or assume either fee applies. Fiscal-year-based deadlines must wait for verified fiscal-year, applicability and extension facts.
